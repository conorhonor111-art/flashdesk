How it works

How to get help on your PC — from start to finish.

This page explains exactly what happens when someone connects to your computer with FlashDesk, what they can do, what they cannot do, and how to close the session when you are done.

FlashDesk will never telephone you.

If someone calls you and asks you to download FlashDesk so they can "fix" your computer, this is a scam. Legitimate helpers only connect when you start the conversation and ask them to help. Hang up.

Step by step

What actually happens during a FlashDesk session.

Five steps — two for your helper, three for you. You are in control at every point.

  1. 1

    Your helper downloads FlashDesk on their computer.

    They go to flashdesk.org and download the same single file you will. No installation, no account. It takes about a minute on most connections.

  2. 2

    You download FlashDesk on your computer.

    Go to flashdesk.org and download the file. Windows may show a SmartScreen warning — this is expected. Our security page explains why and what to click.

    FlashDesk is one .exe file, about 70 MB. Nothing installs. Nothing runs in the background.

  3. 3

    Open FlashDesk — your 9-digit code appears.

    When FlashDesk opens, you will see a large 9-digit number. This is your FlashDesk ID. It changes every time you open the program.

    Your ID — read this to your helper:

  4. 4

    Read your 9-digit code to your helper over the phone.

    Tell your helper the number. They type it into FlashDesk on their end and press Connect. Your computer will receive their request — nothing happens yet.

    Important: FlashDesk shows you your helper's ID number — not their name. Names can be invented. A number comes directly from their copy of FlashDesk and cannot be faked. Before you accept, make sure it matches the number your helper reads back to you.

  5. 5

    A dialog appears — read it, then press Accept.

    FlashDesk shows a dialog with two equally-sized buttons: Accept and Reject. The Accept button is disabled for the first three seconds so you have time to read the dialog. Once you press Accept, your helper can see your screen and move your mouse.

    A coloured bar appears at the top of your screen for the entire session. It cannot be hidden — not even by your helper.

When you are done

Ending the session.

Close FlashDesk or press Disconnect.

When your helper is finished, close the FlashDesk window or press the Disconnect button. The session ends immediately. Your helper's screen goes blank — they can no longer see or control anything.

They cannot reconnect without you accepting again.

Once you close FlashDesk, it is over. Your 9-digit code changes the next time you open the program. If the same person calls back tomorrow and asks to connect, they will need a new code — and you will see another Accept / Reject dialog before anything happens.

Note: if the connection drops and the same helper reconnects within 90 seconds, that reconnect skips the dialog — it is treated as the same session resuming, not a new one.

The file panel can copy a file. It cannot delete, rename or move anything.

What a session can do

Eight things — each one checkable against the code.

Not something the program is meant to do — something it actually does. If a claim could not be verified against the source, it is not on this page.

Nothing installs

It is one file. There is no installer step, and no administrator password is asked for to run it — the program starts with the same permissions as any other window you open.

The number is an address, not a password

Your 9-digit FlashDesk number is how a helper finds you — the way a phone number finds a house. A separate private value, never displayed, is what actually proves ownership of that number. The relay checks it on every connection.

Every new session is accepted by hand

An incoming connection shows a dialog with the caller's number and two equally-weighted buttons, Accept and Reject. While that dialog is open, the connected helper's remote-control input cannot reach it — the person asking for control cannot also press Accept.

A strip stays on screen, and cannot be hidden

While anyone is connected, a coloured band stays on your screen for the whole session. It belongs entirely to the window running on your own computer — there is nothing the connected helper can send that touches it.

A log stays on your computer

Every connection — who asked, when, whether you accepted or refused — is written to a plain text file on your own machine. It does not record your screen, anything you typed, or the contents of any file.

File access is a second, separate question

Accepting a session lets a helper see your screen. It does not let them open your files. File access needs its own separate permission, asked for the first time it is needed.

Copy, never delete or rename

Once file access is granted, a helper can look through your files and copy one to their own computer, or send you one of theirs. There is no delete, rename or move — by design.

An arriving program is named every time

If a helper sends you a program rather than an ordinary file, you are asked about it by name — the file's name and where it would be saved — every single time, even after you have already agreed to file access generally.

Hard limits

What FlashDesk deliberately cannot do.

These are not features that are planned for later — they are design decisions that make the program safer by making certain things structurally impossible.

No background service

FlashDesk only runs while its window is open. Close the window — everything stops. There is no process left running in the background, no way to reconnect silently.

No autostart

FlashDesk does not add itself to Windows' startup list. It does not run when you log in. If you want it to run, you open it yourself.

Closing ends everything

Closing the window ends the session, the screen capture, and the connection — all at once. There is nothing left running afterward. Your helper's view goes blank immediately.

No file deletion or renaming

A helper with file access can copy a file to their computer or send one to you. Delete, rename, and move do not exist as things the file panel can ask your computer to do.

No connecting by name

FlashDesk shows your helper's 9-digit number — not a name. Names can be invented. A 9-digit ID comes from the program itself and cannot be faked. You always know exactly who is asking to connect.

One screen only (for now)

If your computer has more than one screen, your helper currently sees and controls only the one you are on. Switching between screens is planned but not yet built.

Your safety

You always know who is connecting.

FlashDesk shows your helper's ID number — not a name — so you always know who is connecting.

When someone connects to your computer, FlashDesk shows their 9-digit number. That number is generated by their copy of FlashDesk — it is not something they typed or chose. Before you press Accept, compare it with the number your helper reads to you over the phone. If the numbers match, you know you are connecting to the right person.

A name can be invented. A number cannot be faked.

If the number does not match — press Reject.

A mismatch means someone other than your helper is trying to connect to your computer. Reject the connection, close FlashDesk, and call your helper back on a number you know.
