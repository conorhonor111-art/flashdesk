Privacy

This page describes what the code actually does, checked directly against the source rather than written from a template. Conor Honor is not a lawyer, and this is not a legal document — it is a plain description, kept honest by being checkable.

Your screen, your keystrokes and your files are never written to disk — only sent, live, during a session you accepted.

What is kept, and where

What is kept, and where.

Session log

A plain text file on your own computer, nowhere else. It records who connected, when, and what was decided — accepted, refused, or timed out. It stays there unless you separately let your helper copy files off your computer; FlashDesk has no server it reports session history to either way.

Your FlashDesk number

Stored on your own computer. The relay that connects two numbers together knows your number and a separate private value your computer proves ownership with — it does not know your name, and nothing about who you are is attached to it.

This website

Loads nothing from any other server, sets no cookies, and runs no analytics of any kind. There is no script on this site at all — unusual, since most sites load fonts, trackers or analytics from somewhere else, and this one deliberately does not.

What is never recorded

What is never recorded.

During a session, your screen is sent live to whoever you accepted — that is the product. What the code does not do is keep a copy of any of it:

Screen contents

Nothing captured from your screen is written to disk. It exists only for as long as it takes to send it during a live session, then it is gone.

Keystrokes

What is typed during a session is not logged anywhere, by either side.

File contents

If a file is copied during a session, its name is written to your session log — its contents are not.

The program's own internal diagnostics — a capture-health log and a diagnostics report, both for troubleshooting — record only technical numbers (timings, counts, which capture method was used) and never anything you saw or typed.

Who this is shared with

The only computer outside your own that FlashDesk ever talks to is the relay that connects two sessions together — there is no other server, no analytics company, and no third party anywhere in the path. What that relay itself retains about a connection, beyond the moment of connecting two numbers, is not yet documented on this page — see the note below.

Being honest about what is still open

This page describes the client program with real confidence, checked against its source. The relay side of the system — the small server that introduces two FlashDesk numbers to each other — has not yet had the same documentation pass written for exactly what it logs and for how long. Nothing found while checking suggests it does anything beyond what connecting two numbers requires, but "nothing found yet" is not the same promise as a page that has been written and checked line by line the way the rest of this one has. That will be corrected here once it has.
