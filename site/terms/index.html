1. What FlashDesk is

FlashDesk is a remote support tool for Windows. When you run it, it generates a 9-digit ID. Someone who already has your trust — a family member, a friend, a support person you called — can enter that ID on their own computer to see your screen and control your mouse, so they can help you with a problem.

Every session requires your active permission. You see a dialog showing the 9-digit number of the person requesting access. You can end the session at any time by clicking the visible amber strip or closing the window. No background process runs when the session ends, and nothing is left installed on your computer.

Using FlashDesk means you have read these terms and agree to them.

2. What you can and cannot do

Permitted uses

  • Helping someone you know with a computer problem over the phone or a video call.
  • Receiving help from someone you trust, such as a family member or a support person at a company you have contacted yourself.
  • Personal, business, or commercial use — there is no restriction on use case as long as both sides have genuinely consented.

Prohibited uses

  • Deception and fraud. You must not connect to someone's computer under false pretences — for example, by pretending to be a support person from a company you do not work for, or by asking someone to download FlashDesk as part of a scam.
  • Uninvited access. Every session must start with the other person's free and informed consent. Do not enter someone's ID without their knowledge, and do not pressure, trick, or manipulate anyone into giving you access.
  • Illegal activity. Do not use FlashDesk for anything prohibited by the laws of the country where you or the other person are located.
  • Sharing access without consent. Do not allow a third party to use a session the other person did not know would be shared.

FlashDesk will never telephone you unsolicited. If you receive a call from someone claiming to be from FlashDesk, Microsoft, your internet provider, or any other company — and they ask you to download FlashDesk or give them your 9-digit ID — hang up. That is a scam. Legitimate support starts with you making contact, not the other way around.
