WHAT TO WATCH WHILE TWO STRANGERS RUN FLASHDESK
================================================================

You are on the phone. You can hear hesitation they will never describe. Five
things, in the order they happen. Write them down as they occur - afterwards
nobody remembers the pauses.


1. THE DOWNLOAD
   Expect: comes through with no block at all.
   COUNT: does anyone mention a warning while downloading?
   If yes, get the exact words - that would mean GitHub's treatment has changed
   and the page is wrong again.


2. THE SMARTSCREEN SCREEN  <<< THIS IS THE CERTIFICATE DECISION
   This is the one measurement that matters now.

   COUNT, for each person:
     a) Seconds of silence after they open the file.
     b) Did they find "More info" ON THEIR OWN, or did you have to say it?
     c) Did they get through without you talking them through it?

   The trigger: if a tester stops here and does NOT get through on their own,
   that is the signal to buy the certificate. One person failing is the number.

   Do not coach them before they try. Let the silence run. If you rescue them
   too early you destroy the only measurement that decides EUR 209.


3. THE NUMBER
   Expect: a 9-digit number within a few seconds of the window opening.
   COUNT: seconds from opening the file to reading the number aloud.
   Watch for: two people getting the SAME number - that would mean cloned
   machines and the relay will refuse the second one.


4. THE CONSENT DIALOG
   Expect: the caller's number, the line saying they have never connected with
   this number before, and Accept greyed for about three seconds.

   WATCH FOR THE ONE THING THAT MATTERS: did they READ it, or hunt for the
   button? Ask afterwards what it said. If they cannot say, the dialog is
   decoration and the whole safety design rests on nothing.

   Also: did the 3-second delay annoy them, or did they not notice?


5. DURING AND AFTER THE SESSION
   Expect: an orange band on the shared machine the whole time, and the picture
   moving on the other side.

   WATCH FOR:
     - Do they say the picture froze? Roughly how long?
     - Did anyone panic when the mouse moved on its own? (They were warned, but
       being warned and experiencing it are different.)
     - Does closing the window really end it? Ask them to check Task Manager.

   THEN ASK FOR THE FILE - it answers everything else without them describing
   anything:
       C:\Users\<their name>\AppData\Roaming\FlashDesk\sessions.txt

   It should contain ONE block per session, and its "Speed limiting" line is the
   first real evidence of adaptive quality on two genuinely separate home
   connections - the one thing still never tested outside a simulation.


================================================================
THE SINGLE MOST VALUABLE THING YOU CAN BRING BACK

Not the numbers. The place where each person WENT QUIET and what they were
looking at. That is where a stranger who is not on the phone with you gives up
entirely, and it is the only thing this test can measure that nothing else can.
